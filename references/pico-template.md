# PICO proof-of-value template

One single-spaced page, about 400 to 550 words.

**Lead (1 to 2 sentences).** Design, size and what the test checks. Example: "A four-week randomized test with 40 students (20 per group)."

**Test question.** Among [population], does [intervention], compared with [comparison], change [outcomes] within [time frame]?

**Population (P).** Who the test subjects are, how they are recruited, the exact number, and how they are assigned (random, stratified by what).

**Intervention (I).** What the intervention group does, how long setup takes, and where they review the tool's output.

**Comparison (C).** What the control group does instead: their usual process, in the same window and volume. Note whether they get the tool afterwards.

**Outcomes (O).**
- Primary outcome, scored blind if possible.
- Two to four secondary outcomes (time, accuracy, real-world result).
- One spread measure: would recommend, referrals, or repeat use.

**How differences are measured.** Baseline measurement, the test for each outcome (two-sample t-test for averages, chi-square or Fisher's exact test for rates), and a sample size note.

**Expected outcomes.** A number for each outcome, labeled as a hypothesis.

## Sample size rule of thumb

For comparing two group means at 80% power and 5% significance:

n per group ≈ 16 ÷ d², where d = expected difference ÷ standard deviation.

- d = 0.5 (moderate effect): about 64 per group
- d = 0.8 (large effect): about 25 per group
- d = 0.9: about 20 per group
- d = 1.0: about 16 per group

Rates of rare events (interviews, conversions under 15%) need far larger samples. If the test cannot reach them, say the result is directional.
